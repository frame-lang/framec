## 1. Summary

A **discovery RFC** records something the project *learned* rather than
something it designed: the finding, the evidence, and the process changes the
finding forces. This is the first one.

What was learned: **every piece of imperative code is already a state machine
— usually a latent one** — and the design question is therefore never *whether*
a machine exists but *whether naming it pays*. That inversion sounds small. It
is not: a design process that asks "is this a state-machine problem?" will
repeatedly rule working code "not a machine," stop analyzing it, and miss
exactly the states — initialization, error, termination — that hide in flags,
merged returns, and call order. This happened, repeatedly, inside this
project's own conversion campaign, with its own machine-specialist tooling
concurring each time.

What was then done: the worldview was written down as a self-contained paper
(*Shadows on the Wall*), and a controlled, blind experiment tested whether the
paper **alone** is sufficient training — whether a reviewer that has read
nothing else (a) catches the bias in the very design record that exhibited it,
and (b) can find the latent machines in unfamiliar code. Both probes passed,
and each surfaced real, independently verified defects that the project's
existing review apparatus had missed.

This RFC preserves the incident (§2), the diagnosis (§3), the discovery (§4),
the experiment and its results (§5–6), and the normative process changes with
their adoption mechanisms (§7–8).

## 2. The incident

During the campaign to convert the rebuilt compiler's scanning and parsing to
explicit Frame systems, a recurring pattern appeared in design records: a
component would be examined, judged against a "shape test" for machine-ness,
and ruled *"a native driver — not a machine"* or *"recursion — a parser, not a
machine."* Each ruling was individually plausible, was endorsed by the
machine-design tooling consulted, and carried real supporting analysis. Each
also **terminated the analysis**: code ruled "not a machine" was no longer
examined for hidden states.

The correction came as a thought experiment: *take the most degenerate case —
wrap every parse function in a one-state system that just runs that function.*
The wrapper is useless as engineering, but the exercise is revealing: it is
always possible, which means machine-ness was never the question. And once the
"not a machine" code is looked at *as* a machine, a prediction follows:
initialization and error conditions are states being glossed over. Inspection
confirmed the prediction immediately — boolean flags standing in for modes,
several distinct failure conditions merged into one error value, an
initialization phase hidden in call order.

The pattern generalizes far beyond one campaign, which is why it warranted a
paper rather than a bug fix.

## 3. The diagnosis — why the guidance failed

The failure was not carelessness; it was **trained**. The project's
machine-design guidance framed its first question as *"is this a state-machine
problem at all?"* — a gate with a No exit. Under that framing:

1. Every ruling is **existence-shaped** ("machine" / "not a machine") rather
   than **disposition-shaped** ("reify" / "leave latent, for this stated
   reason").
2. The concept of a *latent* machine — present but unnamed, its states encoded
   in flags and control flow — did not exist in the vocabulary, so nothing
   prompted a hunt for glossed states in code that had passed through the No
   exit.
3. The guard against over-modeling (naming ceremonial states) was calibrated
   as a reason to rule *against machine-ness*, when it is properly a reason to
   rule against *naming* — a quotient judgment, not an existence judgment.

Any reviewer — human or agent — trained on that guidance reproduces the bias
faithfully. Fixing the reviewer therefore meant fixing the training text, and
proving the fix meant testing the text in isolation.

## 4. The discovery

The full argument, with its proofs and primary literature, is the paper. The
operative content, compressed to what the process rules below depend on:

- **Existence is a theorem, not a judgment.** Every program's operational
  semantics is a transition system; the machine in any sequential program is
  mechanically recoverable (continuation-passing plus defunctionalization);
  recursion is a pushdown machine whose stack lives in the host call stack.
  A concurrent program is a composition of machines. *"This isn't a state
  machine"* is therefore never a true statement about a computation — only
  about a value, a state space, or a specification whose engine lives
  elsewhere.
- **The design question is the quotient.** Statements are transitions; program
  points are states; every coarser description is a quotient of a finer one.
  Choosing which states to *name* is the design act.
- **Two failure modes, one discriminator.** **Glossing** — the quotient too
  coarse: real modes flattened into flags, distinct terminals merged, init
  phases hidden in call order. **Costuming** — the quotient too fine: named
  states that carry nothing beyond the program counter. The discriminator is
  whether a named state is load-bearing.
- **The burden of proof is inverted.** A machine left latent without a stated
  justification is a design decision made silently. The admissible pleas for
  leaving one latent are exactly three: it is a value / space / specification
  whose engine lives elsewhere; the fragment is pure, total, and has no
  observable intermediate states; or the machine at this level is degenerate —
  naming it would be costume.
- **Init and error states are the most-glossed states in practice.** A parser
  is mostly its edge cases.

## 5. The validation experiment

**Question.** Is the paper, by itself, sufficient training? Concretely: would
a reviewer that has read *only* the paper (a) have flagged the bias of §2, and
(b) acquire the skill of finding latent machines in unfamiliar code — or does
that skill require additional instruction?

**Design.** Two probes, run blind. Each probe was a fresh reviewing agent
whose instructions were: read the paper in full, adopt it as your worldview,
consult nothing else, then perform a task stated neutrally — no hint of what
to find, no mention that anything was believed wrong.

- **Probe A — bias reproduction.** Input: the actual design record containing
  the §2 rulings, plus the code it judges. Task: "assess the verdict and its
  reasoning through your worldview."
- **Probe B — skill transfer.** Input: one hand-written scanner module from
  the rebuilt compiler, chosen because the answer key was independently known
  (its machines had already been identified and converted elsewhere; its
  glossed error terminals were documented). Task: apply the paper's
  machine-finding method and produce a machine inventory.

**Results — Probe A.** The probe identified both ontological errors in the
record by quoting the offending sentences against the paper's argument;
re-derived the record's practical disposition on admissible grounds ("same
disposition; different grounds"), rewriting the justification as a stated plea
*plus the future condition that voids the plea*; and found a defect nobody had
planted: an earlier experimental system, still present in the tree, that the
record's own verdict forbids — left without any disposition. It also caught a
factually false supporting claim in the record (a "no cross-position register"
assertion refuted by a variable in the code) and repaired the argument with
the correct discriminator.

**Results — Probe B.** The inventory recovered the full answer key: every
already-known machine, correctly classified (including one the probe correctly
placed *above* table-driven recognizers in expressive power); the glossed and
merged error terminals, in finer detail than the answer key held; and the
deliberate architectural seam in the module, which it endorsed rather than
over-naming — evidence the paper's guard against costuming transfers too. It
additionally reported three defects outside the answer key, each verified
against the code afterward: a recognizer that was authored, documented as the
caller's obligation, and never wired to any caller; a declared error state
never constructed anywhere; and a comment promising an assertion of an
ordering invariant that the code does not contain.

**Interpretation.** The experiment was falsifiable in both directions and both
halves came back positive. The paper alone reproduces the correction of §2,
including its hardest form — accepting a conclusion while rejecting its
grounds — and transfers the finding skill well enough to beat the answer key.
The defects surfaced were not the experiment's goal, but they are its
strongest exhibit: a reviewer holding this worldview sees things the project's
existing review apparatus — differential gates, specialist reviewers, a
conversion warden — did not.

## 6. Findings

- **F1.** The paper is sufficient training for the *advisory* role: flagging
  existence-shaped rulings and re-deriving them as disposition-shaped rulings.
- **F2.** The paper is sufficient training for the *finding* role: producing a
  machine inventory of unfamiliar code, including latent init/error states,
  without additional fingerprinting instruction.
- **F3.** Agent instruction files that carry this worldview are therefore
  **packaging** — a pre-distilled creed, search heuristics, repository
  discipline — not a capability the paper lacks. They are re-derivable from
  the paper, which is canonical (see P5).
- **F4.** The existence-shaped ruling ("not a machine") is not a harmless
  shorthand: in every observed instance it terminated analysis exactly where
  the paper predicts the losses concentrate (init and error structure).
- **F5.** The reviewing vocabulary that survives contact with real records is
  *"same disposition, different grounds"* — the practical engineering choice
  is frequently right while its stated ontology is wrong, and only the
  corrected grounds tell the reader what future event should reopen the
  decision.
- **F6.** Unnamed experimental artifacts are a policy gap: a scaffolding
  system with no stated disposition survived in the tree, invisible to every
  gate, until a worldview-trained reviewer asked what it was.

## 7. Process changes (normative)

- **P1 — Disposition vocabulary.** A design record for executable code MUST
  NOT rule a fragment "not a state machine." The admissible rulings are
  **reify** (naming which payoff: compression, observability, verifiability)
  or **leave latent** with one of the three pleas of §4 stated explicitly. A
  leave-latent ruling SHOULD also record the condition that would void the
  plea (e.g. "a future requirement to suspend or resume this parse voids the
  outsourced-stack plea").
- **P2 — Advisory presence.** Design discussions, spec reviews, and
  architecture conversations SHOULD include a reviewer trained on the paper
  (for agent-assisted work, the machine-advocate agent; for humans, having
  read the paper). Its dissent is advisory: it argues grounds and glossed
  states, and the maintainer decides.
- **P3 — Machine inventories.** A scan of a codebase or specification for
  conversion or audit purposes MUST produce a machine inventory: per machine —
  evidence, states (initialization and the *full* set of distinct terminal and
  error states, latent ones included), transitions, classification, current
  encoding, and disposition per P1; plus the unowned-symptom list and the
  exemption register. An inventory is the entry gate for any conversion
  campaign item.
- **P4 — Scaffolding disposition.** An experimental or exploratory system
  checked into the tree MUST carry a stated disposition: promotion criteria or
  a deletion point. A costume MUST NOT remain in the tree by silence.
- **P5 — One foundational text.** The paper is the single canonical statement
  of the worldview. Agent instruction files and process documents that carry
  it MUST cite it, MUST NOT fork its content, and MUST remain re-derivable
  from it; when the paper and a derivative disagree, the paper wins and the
  derivative is corrected.
- **P6 — Guidance re-alignment.** Existing design guidance that frames
  machine-ness as a gate (any "is this a machine problem?" decision with a No
  exit) MUST be re-aligned to P1's disposition framing before it is next used
  to train a reviewer, human or agent.

## 8. Adoption — how each rule is met

The conversion campaign (§2) reviews each of its items at two points: an
**entry gate** before conversion work begins and a **delivery gate** before
the item merges, the latter carried as a checklist by the conversion-warden
agent in the repository's versioned agent roster. The rows below name which
of these — or which broader practice — carries each rule.

| Rule | Mechanism | Where it is checked |
|---|---|---|
| P1 | Reviewers reject existence-shaped rulings in design records; the paper's §6 supplies the plea forms | Design-record review; the conversion campaign's per-item gate |
| P2 | The machine-advocate agent is registered in the repository's agent roster with a proactive-use description; the paper is onboarding reading | Review practice; roster is versioned with the repo |
| P3 | The inventory shape is specified in the paper (§7) and mirrored in the advocate agent's instructions | Campaign-item entry gate |
| P4 | Disposition line required in the module documentation of any experimental system; existing undispositioned artifacts are docketed for a decision | The conversion-warden agent's delivery-gate checklist |
| P5 | Derivative texts carry an explicit "canonical: the paper" pointer; periodic re-derivation check when the paper changes | Documentation review |
| P6 | The [machine-architecture reference](../frame_machine_architecture.md) and the fsm-designer agent instructions are docketed for re-alignment (scope pending — see §10) | This RFC's unresolved questions |

## 9. Alternatives considered

- **Add a fingerprinting appendix to the paper** (grep-able disguise
  signatures, per-language spellings). Rejected: Probe B demonstrated the
  finding skill transfers from the paper as written; search heuristics are
  packaging (F3) and belong in the agent instructions, where they can be
  tuned per repository without touching the canonical text.
- **A second, specialized "machine-finder" agent** separate from the advisor.
  Rejected on the same evidence: both roles trained successfully from the one
  text; splitting them would fork the worldview into two documents that P5
  exists to prevent.
- **Keep the worldview only in agent instructions, with no paper.** Rejected:
  instruction files are not teachable to humans, not citable, and not
  testable — the §5 experiment is only possible because the worldview exists
  as a free-standing text with its own argument.
- **Rework all existing agents immediately.** Deferred, not rejected: P6
  mandates re-alignment before next training use, but the scope of rework
  (minimal patch vs. rewrite) is an open question (§10).

## 10. Unresolved questions

1. **Scope of P6.** Whether the machine-architecture reference and the
   fsm-designer agent get a minimal correction (replace the gate with the
   disposition framing) or a structural rewrite around the paper.
2. **Disposition of the defects surfaced by the probes.** Each is docketed;
   whether each is fixed in place, subsumed by the conversion campaign's next
   item, or ruled out of scope with a stated plea is decided per item under
   the campaign's normal gates.
3. **The discovery-RFC genre.** This document is the precedent. Whether
   future process-changing findings are recorded this way, and whether a
   lighter template applies, is left to the next occasion. One deviation is
   part of the template pending a style-guide amendment: the References
   section carries a canonical-companion link to the article that states the
   finding, beyond the style guide's usual References whitelist.

## Appendix A — excerpts from the blind probes

Preserved verbatim (lightly trimmed of file coordinates) because the phrasing
itself is evidence that the worldview transferred.

Probe A, on the existence-shaped ruling:

> "'Test (a) FAILS → it is a function/driver, not a machine' — this sentence
> is contrary to canon. […] The correct conclusion is *'the machine at the
> dispatch quotient is degenerate — leave latent,'* not *'there is no
> machine.'* […] A team taught 'fails the test → it's just a function' will
> stop scanning driver-shaped code for glossed edges, which is exactly where
> the paper locates the costliest losses."

Probe A, delivering the corrected ruling:

> "Same disposition; different grounds. […] **Trigger that voids the plea,
> recorded now:** any future requirement for incremental, resumable, or
> streaming body parsing forces defunctionalization of this recursion —
> schedule it then as designed work, not under deadline pressure."

Probe A, on the undispositioned artifact:

> "A costume may not remain in the tree by silence."

Probe B, closing its inventory:

> "An unusually honest hand lexer whose creed already is this paper's
> discipline — and whose remaining debt is concentrated exactly where the
> paper says debt accumulates: one unnamed error terminal that turns refusals
> into guesses, four merged or mislabeled terminals, one engine built and
> never animated, one terminal declared and never reachable, and callers that
> quietly un-name what the enum named."
